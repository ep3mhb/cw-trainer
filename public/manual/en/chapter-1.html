Chapter 1Welcome

RESONIK CW Trainer is a web application for learning and practising Morse code (CW). It teaches you to copy by ear with the Koch method, measures your sending on a real key, lets you work a simulated operator through a complete contact, and — once you sign in — puts you on a shared virtual band where you can work other members, live, with your own key.

This manual describes every part of the application as it stands in version 0.15.5. You do not need to read it from front to back: Chapter 2 gets you started in five minutes, and the rest is there for when you want to know what a control does or why it behaves the way it does.

1.1What it is, and what it is not

Everything in the first six tabs — Receive, Send, QSO, Progress, Settings and Help — runs in your browser. No account is needed for any of it, and after the page has loaded once it works without any connection at all. Your progress is stored on the device you are using and nowhere else.

There is one exception, and it is better named here than discovered later: the final exam, the one that completes the course and earns a certificate, is set and marked by the server. Chapter 4 explains how, and why it is the only part of your practice that leaves the device.

What the server knows about you

The privacy notice at www.mikroutik.com/privacy.html lists everything the server holds, why, and for how long. It is written to be read rather than agreed to, and it is linked from Help → About as well.

Beyond that, only the On air tab talks to the server, and only after you sign in. Even there, no audio ever travels: your key-down and key-up moments are sent, and the tone is generated again in the other member’s browser. Nothing goes on the air in the radio sense. Two browsers are talking to each other, so no licence is needed for any of it.

1.2Where to find it and what you need

Open https://cw.mikroutik.com in a current browser: Safari, Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a computer, a tablet or a phone. Headphones or speakers are all you need to start; a key or paddle is optional, and Chapter 5 explains how to connect one.

ComputerAny recent browser. A keyboard is a perfectly good paddle: [ is dit and ] is dah.Phone or tabletThe same page. The key pad on screen becomes a touch paddle — left half dit, right half dah.SoundWired headphones or the built-in speakers. Bluetooth is fine for listening but adds a delay that spoils sending practice (see 10.3).AccountOnly for the On air tab and the final exam; managed under Settings. Name, email address and a password of at least ten characters; your callsign if you hold one.

1.3Installing it like an app

The application installs on your home screen or dock and then opens without the browser furniture around it. It is the same application and the same stored progress either way; installing only changes how you reach it.

  1. iPhone or iPad: open the page in Safari, tap the Share button, then “Add to Home Screen”.
  2. Android: Chrome shows an install prompt, or use the browser menu → “Install app”.
  3. Desktop Chrome or Edge: an install icon appears at the right of the address bar, or look under the browser menu for “Install RESONIK CW Trainer”.
Working offline

Offline use needs the version served from the web address, not a file opened from your disk. Load the page once while connected and it works without a connection after that. If it does not, something is clearing your browsing data when the browser quits — “clear on exit” settings and some privacy extensions do this, and they take your practice history with them.

1.4How this manual is written

Names of tabs, buttons and controls appear in bold exactly as they read on screen. Keys you press are shown like Shift. Things sent in Morse are shown in code. Boxed notes carry three labels: a tip you can skip, a note worth reading once, and a caution that saves you from a real mistake.

Amber is the colour of the panel: an amber lamp, badge or trace means “live” or “yours”; green means “good” or “connected”; red means “stop” or “something is wrong”.
