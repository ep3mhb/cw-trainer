A prosign is sent as one unbroken run with no gap inside — that is what makes it punctuation rather than two letters side by side. In writing they are shown with a bar over the top or, as here and in the application, in angle brackets.

10.2Ending a transmission: K, KN, AR and SK

This is where nearly every beginner mistake happens, because all four mean roughly “I have stopped sending” and they are not interchangeable.

SendIt meansUse it when
KOver — anyone may reply.At the end of a CQ call, when you want any station at all to come back to you.
KNOver — named station only.Mid-contact, handing back to the station you are working. Keeps others from breaking in.
AREnd of message.Finishing a transmission that does not invite an immediate reply — typically when calling a specific station that has not yet answered. Usually sent as "AR K".
SKEnd of contact.Your very last transmission of the QSO, after 73. Not before.
In one sentence

K after a CQ, KN for the rest of the contact, SK once at the very end.

Every prosign, and when to send it

ProsignSoundMeansSend it when
KN−·−−·Over — but only the station I named may reply.Handing over inside a contact, when you do not want a third station joining in. This is the one to use for most of a QSO.
AR·−·−·End of message. Nothing more to say in this transmission.Closing a transmission where no particular reply is invited yet — for example when calling a specific station that has not answered you. Often followed immediately by K.
BT−···−Separator — a new paragraph. Written as "=".Between the parts of your transmission: after the greeting, after the signal report, after the name. Also the polite thing to send while you think, instead of going silent.
SK···−·−End of contact. This exchange is finished for good.On your final transmission only, after the 73. Sending it earlier tells the other operator you are leaving, which is rarely what a beginner means.
AS·−···Wait, stand by.When you need a few seconds — someone at the door, a note to find. Seconds, not minutes; for a longer wait send QRX with a time.
VE···−·Understood, received correctly.Acknowledging something specific. Much less common than a plain R.
SOS···−−−···Distress. A life is in danger.Never in practice, and never on the air unless it is real. It is in this table so you recognise it if you ever hear it.

Mistakes worth avoiding

  • Sending SK in the middle of a contact. It means you are leaving, and a polite operator will simply say 73 and go. Use KN to hand back instead.
  • Sending K instead of KN during a QSO, then wondering why a third station replied. K invites everybody.
  • Sending the letters separately. A R with a normal gap between them is the word "AR", not the prosign — the whole point is that the elements run together with no gap, so it sounds like one long character.
  • Leaving out BT entirely, so the transmission arrives as one unbroken wall. The separators are what make a message readable at the other end.
  • Sending 73 more than once, or sending "73s". It is already plural — it means "best regards".

10.3Q codes

Three-letter codes that ask a question with a “?” after them and state a fact without one. They exist because they are quick to send and mean the same thing in every language.

CodeAs a questionAs a statement
QRLIs this frequency busy?This frequency is busy.
QRMAre you being interfered with?I am being interfered with by another station.
QRNAre you troubled by static?I am troubled by static and atmospheric noise.
QROShall I increase power?Increase power.
QRPShall I decrease power?Decrease power. (Also: operating at low power.)
QRQShall I send faster?Send faster.
QRSShall I send more slowly?Send more slowly. The most useful code a learner can send.
QRTShall I stop sending?I am closing down.
QRUHave you anything for me?I have nothing for you.
QRVAre you ready?I am ready.
QRXWhen will you call again?Wait — stand by.
QRZWho is calling me?You are being called by…
QSBAre my signals fading?Your signals are fading.
QSLCan you acknowledge receipt?I acknowledge receipt. (Also: a confirmation card.)
QSOCan you contact… directly?A contact between two stations.
QSYShall I change frequency?Change to another frequency.
QTHWhat is your location?My location is…
QTRWhat is the correct time?The time is…
