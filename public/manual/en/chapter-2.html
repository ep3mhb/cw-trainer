Chapter 2Getting started in five minutes

In this chapter
  1. 2.1The welcome page

2.1The welcome page

The first time you open the application on a device, before any of the tabs, you are shown a page that says what this is. Behind the words a band scope runs, and a station on it is calling — CQ CQ DE CW TRAINER K — so the first Morse you meet here is Morse being sent and read, with the letters appearing above it as they go.

Beneath the band is the whole course: forty characters, K and M lit and the other thirty-eight waiting. It is an honest picture of the undertaking, and a more useful one than a sentence saying “forty characters”. Below that is one button, Start lesson 1, three short routes in for the three people who tend to arrive — somebody new to Morse, somebody returning after years away, somebody running a class — and the manual in its three languages.

When you will and will not see it

It appears only on a browser holding no practice history, with nobody signed in — that is, only to somebody genuinely new to that device. Once you have answered a single drill you go straight to where you left off, as before. You can open it again whenever you like from Help → About, which is also how to show it to somebody else.

Opening it makes no request of any kind: no typeface fetched from elsewhere, no image, no counter. That is deliberate and it is checked before each release, because the privacy notice says that somebody without an account talks to nothing at all, and a welcome page is exactly the sort of page that quietly stops that being true.

2.2First run

  1. Press “Enable sound” in the header. Every browser blocks audio until you interact with the page; this is that interaction. The Audio lamp turns green and reads “running”.
  2. Go to Receive and press “Start session”. You will hear only two characters, K and M, sent at a realistic speed with long gaps between them. That is deliberate — see Chapter 3.
  3. Type what you heard, then press Enter. Do not try to get all of it. Copy what you catch and let the rest go: chasing a character you missed usually costs you the next three.
  4. Finish the ten drills. At 90% or better a third character unlocks. Below that, run the session again.
  5. Look at Progress. The confusion matrix shows which characters you are mixing up. That is what to drill next.
Tip

Fifteen minutes a day beats two hours on a Sunday. This is ear training, and ears learn from repetition spread out over days, not from cramming.

2.3The header plate

SpeedYour current character speed and effective speed, as “18 / 8 WPM”. Change them on the Receive tab.CourseHow many characters your course has unlocked so far, out of forty.AudioThe state of the sound engine. Green “running” is what you want. If it reads anything else, press the button beside it.Enable sound / Restart audioThe same button under two names. It says “Enable sound” until audio has started once, and “Restart audio” after that. Press it whenever the sound goes away — for example after changing headphones.

2.4The tabs

TabWhat it is forNeeds
ReceiveThe Koch course and copying drills. This is where most of your time goes.Nothing
SendKeying practice against a target, with a timing report on what you sent.A key, or the keyboard
QSOA complete contact with a simulated operator, in band noise, typed or keyed.Nothing
On airThe virtual band: sign in, tune, call CQ, work other members live.An account and a connection
ProgressEverything the application has measured about you, with CSV export.Nothing
SettingsAudio, key mapping, and your data (export, import, delete).Nothing
HelpA shorter version of this manual, always at hand, with a clickable Morse chart.Nothing

The address bar remembers which tab you are on (#receive, #air and so on), so a bookmark to the On air tab opens straight onto the band.
