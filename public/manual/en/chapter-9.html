9.1Audio

Sidetone pitchThe tone you hear, in hertz. 500–700 Hz is usual. On the band, a station sounds at this pitch when you are zero-beat with it.Output volumeThe application’s own level, separate from your device volume.Envelope rise and fallHow long each tone takes to rise and to fall, in milliseconds. Default 6 ms; the useful range is 4 to 8. Switching a tone on instantly sounds like a click at the start of every element, and an hour of that is tiring to copy. Measured on this application at 6 ms, the spread 1 kHz either side of the tone sits 84 dB below it; switched hard it would be 56 dB — nearly 300 times stronger. Past about 10 ms the edges start to soften the rhythm you are trying to hear.WeightingThe dah-to-dit ratio the application sends with. 3.0 is textbook; slightly heavier is often easier to copy.Latency warning aboveThe threshold, in ms, at which the Send tab’s latency panel turns red.Test — send V V VPlays three Vs so you can check pitch and volume.Mute / UnmuteSilences the application without touching your device.

9.2Real conditions

A panel of its own, and one you will not see at first: it appears once you have unlocked ten characters. Fading, static, another station and a hand at the key, at off, mild or strong, with each of the four switchable under that. 3.7 explains what each one does and why practising against them is worth the trouble; this is only where they live.

It does not touch your examinations

Whatever is set here, the per-character exam and the final exam both play a clean signal. Nothing you choose on this panel can make your certificate mean something different from anybody else’s.

9.3Key mapping

The default is the VBand convention: [ for dit, ] for dah, and the left and right mouse buttons. Set dit, Set dah and Set straight key each capture whatever you press next — a keyboard key or a mouse button — and Escape cancels. Restore VBand defaults puts the brackets back.

9.4Your data

Export everythingWrites a single file with your settings, course position and all history to your downloads. This is your backup.Import a fileRestores such a file — for example on a new computer.Roll up old dataCondenses drill-by-drill detail older than 30 days into summaries, keeping the statistics but shrinking the store. The application does this by itself once per load.Delete all local dataRemoves everything stored on this device and reloads. It does not touch your On air account, nor any training records you have asked the server to keep — both live on the server, and are removed from Your account.

9.5Your account: profile and password

Your profile — QTH, grid square, rig, antenna, licence, preferred sidetone and target speed — lives under Your account on the Settings tab. It is the one place these are kept: the sidetone you save there becomes the sidetone this browser uses; the QSO tab’s robot addresses you by your own callsign or training identifier once you have signed in. On the band, whatever you filled in is shown under your callsign to anyone who can hear you, as you would send it in the exchange. Leave a field blank and nothing is shown. Press Save profile when done.

To change your password, give the current one and a new one of at least ten characters, then press Change password. Changing or resetting it signs out every other device you were signed in on. Sign out at the very bottom leaves the band and forgets your session on this device.

9.6Keeping your training records on the server

Everything you practise stays in the browser you practised in. That is the default, it is what the application has always done, and for most people it is right. It has one consequence: drill on a telephone and at a desk and you have two unrelated histories, neither of which is your progress — and clearing your browsing data takes the lot.

So you may ask for a summary of each session to be kept on the server. It is off until you switch it on: there is a checkbox when you register, and a control under Your account afterwards. Having an account does not switch it on.

Two things follow. Your Progress tab grows a panel called Across your devices, which is the same report drawn from every machine you practise on. And when you sign in somewhere else, the application takes whichever course position is further on — this browser’s or the server’s — so a lesson you unlocked on the train is where you begin at the desk. It never moves you backwards: a character you have unlocked stays unlocked, whichever machine earned it.

What is sentFor each finished session: when it began and ended, the kind of drill, both speeds, how many characters it asked of you, how many you got right, and your course position at the time. Within the session, per character: how often it came up, how often you were right, and your total reply time for the ones you were right about. Also the characters a failed exam asked you to practise more.What is never sentYour keystrokes. What you typed, character by character, stays in this browser and goes nowhere — it is the closest thing this application holds to a recording of somebody fumbling. Nothing from the Send tab or the band is included either.Who can see itYou. Not another member, and not an administrator.Switching it offDeletes what the server holds, at once. The nightly backups are kept for fourteen days, so it leaves those within a fortnight. Switch it on again later and it starts empty.If you are offlineA session finished without a connection goes up the next time the application can reach the server. There is nothing to press, and nothing is lost in the meantime.
Closing your account

Training records are deleted with the account, like the exam and the certificate. Your practice history in this browser is untouched.
